# Dense Karplus--Strong vibrato/bend inverse experiment

## What we tested

This experiment asked a deliberately narrow question: if a target sound is
generated by our own Karplus--Strong renderer, can gradient descent recover the
dense synthesis controls that produced it?

This is an optimization-closure experiment, not an encoder experiment. There
is no neural network predicting controls from audio. Instead, the controls
themselves are optimized directly against one synthetic four-second A2 guitar
gesture.

The gesture contains:

- one noise-excitation frame near 0.1 seconds;
- a smoothly introduced 5 Hz, ±30-cent vibrato from 0.4 to 2.0 seconds;
- a minimum-jerk bend from 0 to +200 cents between 2.0 and 3.2 seconds;
- a held +200-cent pitch for the remainder of the clip.

The target uses constant decay `0.99`, loop damping `0.2`, pluck position
`0.25`, and dynamics `0.9`.

## What we implemented

We added a separate time-domain `DenseKSSynth`, leaving the existing
event-based `Synth` unchanged. It accepts six 250-frame controls:

1. relative noise gate;
2. desired delay length in samples;
3. decay coefficient;
4. loop-filter damping;
5. pluck position;
6. dynamics.

The renderer uses deterministic white noise, zero-order hold for the gate, and
linear interpolation for the remaining controls. Gate magnitude is normalized
before synthesis, so the optimizer cannot use an implicit global gain. The
delay remains the desired loop period and uses the same fifth-order Lagrange
interpolation and loop-filter phase compensation as the existing renderer.

We also implemented a differentiable version of the canonical joint
time--frequency sliced Wasserstein metric. It uses a globally normalized Hann
magnitude spectrogram, ten fixed 2D projections, and 512 weighted quantiles.
Silence and exact self-comparison have explicit finite behavior.

Every predicted waveform is RMS-matched to the target inside its audio loss.
MSS and 2D-OT are separately normalized by their initial RMS gradient with
respect to rendered audio. This makes the hybrid coefficient of `0.01` a real
relative gradient weight.

## Experiment design

We compared three objectives:

- normalized MSS plus gate sparsity;
- normalized 2D-OT plus gate sparsity;
- normalized 2D-OT plus `0.01` normalized MSS plus gate sparsity.

For each objective, we tested sparsity weights `0.001`, `0.01`, and `0.1`, with
seeds `2027`, `2028`, and `2029`. This produced 27 optimization runs. Each run
used 1000 Adam steps, cosine learning-rate decay from `0.03` to `0.003`, and
gradient-norm clipping at 10.

The run system writes exact resumable checkpoints, raw and RMS-matched audio,
controls, optimizer histories, metrics, spectrograms, and control plots.

## Execution and verification

The source was run from commit `028459b7` on the
`experiment/dense-vibrato-bend-ot` branch.

Before the sweep, an A100 preflight required:

- an `sm_80` CUDA device;
- pinned `torchlpc==0.7.2`;
- `torchlpc.EXTENSION_LOADED == True`;
- finite CUDA forward and backward passes.

The final preflight passed with PyTorch 2.10, CUDA 12.9, and an NVIDIA A100 PCIe
40 GB. All 27 fits completed successfully, and all 10 focused renderer and OT
tests passed. The median optimization time was about 418 seconds per fit. The
parallel sweep took approximately 45 minutes of wall time and 3.11 aggregate
GPU-hours.

## Main results

The table reports the best sparsity setting within each loss family according
to the planned ranking: median delay error first, then gate sparsity and MSS.

| Loss family | Best λ | Whole-clip delay median / mean | Vibrato mean | Bend mean | Effective gate frames | Raw MSS | Canonical 2D-OT |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MSS | 0.1 | 5.19 / 67.10 cents | 3.06 | 95.40 | 13.61 | 124.17 | 115.73 |
| Hybrid | 0.001 | 26.86 / 72.88 cents | 16.54 | 84.75 | 13.59 | 198.17 | 17.65 |
| 2D-OT | 0.1 | 41.42 / 77.76 cents | 24.16 | 93.59 | 19.44 | 300.39 | 17.30 |

MSS with `λ=0.1` ranked first. It recovered the vibrato accurately and produced
the strongest waveform agreement. The OT objectives achieved much lower OT
distance, as expected, but sacrificed delay and waveform recovery. The hybrid
sat between MSS and pure OT: it improved delay recovery and sparsity relative
to pure OT but did not match MSS.

MSS and the hybrid recovered the gate onset exactly; the best pure-OT setting
had a median onset error of 48 ms. However, none of the methods recovered the
one-frame target excitation: the best effective gate counts remained around
13--19 frames.

## Interpretation

The experiment demonstrates partial, rather than complete, optimization
closure.

The early pitch trajectory and vibrato can be recovered well with MSS, but the
bend remains poorly identified. The small whole-clip median delay error for MSS
hides large errors later in the clip: its median bend-region error is still
about 95 cents. The optimizer can trade delay against decay and damping while
maintaining a plausible audio fit.

Pluck position and dynamics also remained close to their initialization rather
than their targets. This agrees with their weak identifiability once the
excitation has ended. The broad recovered gate gives the optimizer further
freedom to explain the same waveform using a different control trajectory.

The key conclusion is therefore not that dense inversion is solved. Instead:

- the renderer and all three objectives support stable end-to-end gradients;
- MSS is the strongest of the tested losses for recovering this pitch gesture;
- canonical 2D-OT and waveform/pitch accuracy describe genuinely different
  notions of fit;
- the current sparsity term is insufficient to recover a one-frame excitation;
- additional structure or supervision is needed for bend, gate, and timbre
  control identifiability.

## Outputs

Results are under `experiments/outputs/dense-vibrato-bend-ot/`:

- `summary.csv`: all 27 per-run results;
- `group_summary.csv`: median/IQR summaries and requested ranking;
- `pareto_mss.png` and `pareto_ot.png`: audio-fit versus gate-sparsity plots;
- `target.wav`: matched-renderer target;
- one directory per arm, sparsity weight, and seed containing raw/matched WAVs,
  controls, histories, checkpoints, metrics, and figures.
